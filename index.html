Allergen Matrix

Paste a recipe. Get an allergen report where every result points at the line it came from, and anything the table can't place is marked for review instead of guessed.

Nothing leaves your device. The report is built in your browser from the recipe and a fixed ingredient table — there is no AI model in the loop, so it cannot invent an allergen.

How to read the report

Every allergen gets one of three values, and only three:

ValueWhat it means
PresentAn ingredient matched a table row that assigns this allergen. Butter is milk, by definition.
UndeterminedAn ingredient may contain it, depending on brand, recipe, or processing. Soy sauce is usually brewed with wheat — but not always.
Not foundNothing in the listed ingredients indicated it. This is not a claim that the dish is free of it.

If any ingredient isn't in the table, the whole report turns provisional and every “not found” row says which ingredient is still open. An unidentified ingredient could contain anything.

The review queue is the useful part. It's the short list someone has to check: a supplier label, a brand, a house recipe, or a vague ingredient to pin down. On a 35-line recipe it's usually two or three items.

Why it reports less than other tools

A free allergen checker, given the meatloaf recipe below, reported six allergen groups — including celery and sesame. Neither word appears anywhere in that recipe. Its own evidence pointed at fragments of unrelated directions, and both allergens were carried into a suggested “Contains:” label.

This page reports three allergens for that recipe, flags two more as undetermined, and hands you three things to check. Every line of that is traceable. A fast wrong answer and a slower right one are not interchangeable when someone is deciding what a guest can eat.

What it does not do

  • Cross-contact, shared equipment, and preparation environment: not assessed.
  • Supplier labels and brand formulations: not read. That's what the review queue is for.
  • Sulphite concentration: recipes never state it, so that row reflects ingredient type only.
  • Allergens outside the US FDA 9 and EU/UK 14: recorded as out-of-scope notes, never placed in the matrix.
  • Spelling: never corrected. If the recipe says “2 dashs,” the report says “2 dashs.”
  • Ingredients named only in the directions or notes are caught only if the table already knows them. A vague one — “add other vegetables as desired” — passes unnoticed here, where a person reading the recipe would stop and ask.

What comes out is a reviewed draft. It is not an allergen certification, and what a restaurant tells a guest stays a human decision.
